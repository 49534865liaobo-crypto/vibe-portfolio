YueTie Air-Ground Robotics

A CTO role shaping air-ground collaborative robotics for confined spaces, infrastructure inspection, emergency response, industrial scenarios and safety-critical deployment logic.

Case summary

Problem

High-risk spaces are hard to inspect

No GPS, poor lighting, confined structures, weak signal and high access risk make conventional inspection inefficient or unsafe. Follow-up can also fragment after a defect is found.

Build

Perception-linked control concept

AINO agent orchestration, no-GPS SLAM, multi-sensor collision avoidance, AI defect recognition, 3D modelling and inventory workflows connect sensing to action.

Value

Safety robotics operating model

The CTO role moves robotics from a demonstration mindset toward scenario libraries, field workflows and a management loop that can alert, assign, verify and archive work.

From inspection to closed-loop response

Perceive

Map difficult environments

No-GPS SLAM supports positioning and real-time 3D mapping in dark, enclosed and signal-poor spaces. Multi-sensor collision avoidance supports navigation through narrow and structurally complex areas.

Coordinate

Connect agents to field work

The AINO intelligent agent engine is described as orchestrating alerts, work orders, intervention, verification, archiving and PDF reporting. Air-ground collaboration brings drones, wheeled platforms, robotic dogs and arms into one scenario model.

Inspect

Make evidence more usable

AI imaging can support recognition of cracks, corrosion, leakage and deformation. The scenario library is described as covering 8 application domains and 31 use cases, including infrastructure, rail, energy, industrial and emergency work.

Evidence

Scenario direction, not outcome data

The evidence supports the role, capabilities and scenario library described in the portfolio. No field-performance metric, deployment approval or independent validation is claimed here.

Limitations

Robotics still needs governance

Competent operators, procedures, emergency controls, maintenance, cybersecurity awareness, privacy controls and human validation of AI-recognised defects remain necessary.
