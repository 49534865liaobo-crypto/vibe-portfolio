What should a corporate AI safety readiness review include?

A readiness review should show whether the organisation has the right use cases, governance, data, people capability and assurance routines to adopt AI safely.

By Alvin Liao, last reviewed 20 Aug 2026

Short answer

The review should cover seven areas: strategic intent, use-case value, risk level, data readiness, governance, people capability and pilot feasibility. The final output should be a simple executive story backed by a practical 90-day roadmap.

Seven review areas

  • Strategic intent: what business and safety outcome AI should support.
  • Use cases: where AI creates real value, not only novelty.
  • Risk level: consequence of wrong, biased or unsupported outputs.
  • Data readiness: availability, sensitivity, quality and ownership.
  • Governance: decision rights, review duties and prohibited uses.
  • People capability: readiness of EHS, operations, HR/L&D and innovation teams.
  • Pilot feasibility: timeline, scope, metrics and implementation support.

What leaders should receive

Leaders should receive a concise readiness narrative, not only a long assessment. The best output explains where to start, where not to start, who owns the pilot, what controls are needed and how success will be measured.

Why 90 days matters

A 90-day roadmap creates enough time for discovery, prototype, user feedback and governance review without turning the work into a slow transformation programme. It also helps leaders make a funding decision based on evidence.

Evidence and official sources

These primary official sources provide public context for readiness, AI governance and risk review:

  • NIST AI Risk Management Framework - official framework overview for identifying and managing AI risks.
  • ISO/IEC 42001:2023 AI management systems - ISO's public overview of organization-level AI management systems.
  • ISO 31000:2018 Risk management - Guidelines - ISO's public overview of organization-wide risk management principles and guidance.
  • EU Artificial Intelligence Act on EUR-Lex - official legal text to consult when assessing whether EU rules apply.

These sources do not certify readiness or determine legal applicability. No paid standard text is reproduced.

Limitations and boundaries

A readiness review is a point-in-time diagnostic, not certification, a legal opinion or a guarantee of safe deployment. It cannot replace risk assessment, system testing, worker consultation, regulatory advice or accountable approval. Timeline and outputs depend on scope, evidence and access.
